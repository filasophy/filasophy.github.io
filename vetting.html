Case study

From no inventory to a working approval process

An independent school, three divisions

Recognized by The 100: Independent School Innovators 2026

The problem

No tool inventory, no shared criteria, no one in charge of approvals. Teachers downloaded what they liked; nobody could say what student data went where. About 150 apps sat in the device management system; the other 150 I had to dig up by hand.

The first attempt failed

I built a detailed implementation guide, and teachers stopped using it. It asked for too many steps. I pulled it before the wider rollout. Every click you add costs you people.

The rebuild

I started over with less friction: one intake form, AI assistants that run the first privacy and fit review, and a tech team review for security and integration. Every decision gets logged with its conditions attached.

The result

301
tools audited
158
removed, rejected, or declined; more than half the stack
149
high-risk apps off student devices in one coordinated cleanup

Approved tools now carry review dates, grade-level limits, and a published safe-apps list for each grade.

What I'd tell another school: the first version of your process will be too heavy. Plan to cut it.
